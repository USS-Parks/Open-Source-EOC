# Tabletop exercise for a small EOC

A two and a half hour discussion-based exercise for the staff of a small
tribal or rural EOC, run in Open Source EOC with the starter pack's
**Tabletop exercise (small EOC)** template. It follows the Homeland Security
Exercise and Evaluation Program (HSEEP): objectives, modules with injects and
discussion questions, a hotwash, and an after-action report with an
improvement plan. It names no hazard: bring any scenario, including one of
this project's exercise scenarios, and fill the slots below from it.

## Before the day

- Choose the scenario and fill **The scenario slots** below. Keep it to one
  operational period.
- Seat six players in the template's positions: Incident Commander (EOC
  Director), Public Information Officer, Operations, Planning and Logistics
  Section Chiefs, and Community Liaison. One person may hold two positions;
  say so at the start.
- Name a facilitator, who reads the injects and keeps time, and at least one
  evaluator, who records against the objectives and plays no part.
- Enter each player as a contact with their position on **Contacts**, so the
  **Exercise players** group holds them.
- Brief the ground rules in writing beforehand.

## Ground rules

- No fault. The exercise tests plans and the tool, not people.
- Players act in their real roles with the authority they really hold.
- Every message, post and product carries the word EXERCISE.
- Nothing leaves the room. When activating, leave **Notify people when it
  activates** unchecked, and send no mass notification, email or text to anyone outside the players.
- When the scenario and a real event collide, the real event wins and the
  exercise stops.

## Objectives

1. Activate the EOC from the tabletop template and fill each position within
   30 minutes of the start.
2. Issue a first public message, marked EXERCISE, within 60 minutes of the
   first inject, and name how it reaches people without phone or internet.
3. Record every at-risk household the scenario names on the welfare checks
   board, and route each that needs help to Operations.
4. Take each resource the scenario needs to a named supplier or a recorded
   gap on the resource requests board.
5. Produce the exercise situation report before the hotwash.

Core capabilities exercised: Operational Coordination; Public Information and
Warning; Mass Care Services; Logistics and Supply Chain Management;
Situational Assessment.

## The scenario slots

Fill these from the chosen scenario before the day:

| Slot | What to write |
|---|---|
| Hazard and onset | What happens and when, and how much warning there is |
| Affected area | Which communities, roads and facilities |
| Households | At least three at-risk households with different needs (for example power for medical equipment, no transport, an elder living alone) |
| Resources | At least two things the EOC must find (for example a generator, a crew, water) |
| A rumor | One piece of misinformation spreading in the community |
| A failure | One loss the EOC must work around (a road, a phone system, a person) |

## Setup in Open Source EOC (10 minutes)

1. On **Incident Setup**, activate **Tabletop exercise (small EOC)** with
   **Incident type** set to **Exercise**, a name starting with "EXERCISE:",
   and **Notify people when it activates** unchecked.
2. Each player signs in, takes their position and opens **Tasks**: each
   position has its exercise checklist.
3. The facilitator records each inject on the **Significant Events** board as
   it is read, so the timeline builds as the exercise runs.

## Module 1: warning and activation (0 to 30 minutes)

Inject 1: the hazard begins, from the **Hazard and onset** slot.

- Who decides to activate, and on what information?
- Who is notified first, and how, if the usual way is down?
- Which positions are filled, and who holds two?

Inject 2: the **A rumor** slot reaches the EOC.

- What does the first public message say, and who clears it?
- How does it reach elders, people without phone or internet, and people who
  do not answer unknown numbers?

## Module 2: people and resources (30 to 90 minutes)

Inject 3: the households from the **Households** slot.

- Operations enters each on the welfare checks board, or a field player uses
  the **Welfare check** form in **Smart Forms**. Which are checked first, and
  why?
- One household cannot be reached. What happens next, and who decides?
- What does the **Welfare follow-up** report show, and who reads it?

Inject 4: the needs from the **Resources** slot.

- Logistics enters each on the resource requests board. Who supplies each?
  What if no one can?
- What does the next operational period need that this one does not?

## Module 3: holding on (90 to 120 minutes)

Inject 5: the loss from the **A failure** slot.

- What stops working, and what replaces it?
- Which decisions so far change?

Planning writes the exercise situation report; the Incident Commander approves
it.

## Hotwash (120 to 150 minutes)

Each player, then each evaluator, answers in turn:

- What went well that we should keep doing?
- What would we do differently, and what would it take?
- What in the tool helped, and what got in the way?

The facilitator records each answer in the activity log.
